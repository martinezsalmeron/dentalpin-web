Three things, in this order: the injured person notifies the employer as soon as practicable, the employer takes immediate steps to get them medical advice and makes any advised treatment available, and then the employer records the incident, investigates its cause and acts to stop it happening again. That is not best practice, it is regulations 7 and 8 of the Health and Safety (Sharp Instruments in Healthcare) Regulations 2013. The part that decides whether any of it works is written beforehand: the procedure has to exist before the day it is needed.

> **These regulations apply to a practice, not just a hospital.** Regulation 3 applies them to "a healthcare employer", defined in regulation 2 as "an employer whose main activity is the management, organisation and provision of healthcare". A two-surgery practice with one employed nurse is inside that definition.

## The duty after an injury is split into two halves

Regulation 7 reads as one paragraph but does two different jobs, and practices tend to do the second and forget the first.

The first half, regulation 7(1), is about the practice. Where an employer is notified of an incident in which an employee has been injured by a medical sharp, the employer must "record the incident", "investigate the circumstances and cause of the incident" and "take any necessary action to prevent a recurrence".

The second half, regulation 7(2), is about the person, and it only bites where the sharp "exposed, or may have exposed, the employee to a biological agent". Then the employer must "take immediate steps to ensure that the employee receives medical advice", must "ensure that any treatment advised by a registered medical practitioner, including post-exposure prophylaxis, is made available to the employee", and must "consider providing the employee with counselling".

Who decides whether prophylaxis is appropriate is a registered medical practitioner, not the practice. What the practice owes is speed and access, which is why the written procedure has to name where the person goes and which number is called.

## What has to be in place before it happens

- **Needles are not capped after use.** Regulation 5(1)(c) prohibits it unless that act is required to control a risk identified by a risk assessment and "the risk of injury to employees is effectively controlled by the use of a suitable appliance, tool or other equipment".
- **Safer sharps, so far as is reasonably practicable.** Regulation 5(1)(b), with "safer sharp" defined in regulation 2 as one "designed and constructed to incorporate a feature or mechanism which prevents or minimises the risk of accidental injury".
- **Written instructions and containers, close to the chair.** Regulation 5(1)(d) requires both "written instructions for employees" and "clearly marked and secure containers" to be "located close to areas where medical sharps are used at work".
- **The policies get reviewed.** Regulation 5(2) requires review "at suitable intervals" so the arrangements "remain up to date and effective".

## The sequence, in order

1. **Deal with the wound, then tell someone.** Regulation 8(1)(a) puts this on the injured person: they must "as soon as practicable" notify their employer, or any other employee with specific responsibility for health and safety, of the incident.
2. **Get the medical advice moving.** Regulation 7(2)(a) is the employer's duty of immediacy, and it is worded as steps to ensure the employee "receives medical advice", not as a decision the practice makes.
3. **Collect the facts of the incident.** Regulation 8(1)(b) requires the injured person to "provide when requested by that employer sufficient information as to the circumstances of the incident" so that the employer can comply with regulation 7.
4. **Record and investigate.** Regulation 7(1) again. The record and the investigation are separate things, and the investigation is the one that changes anything.
5. **Decide whether RIDDOR applies.** This is a different set of regulations with different triggers, and the table below is the whole of it.

## When RIDDOR turns a sharps injury into a report

| | What triggers it | Deadline | Who it goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over-seven-day absence, reg. 4(2) | Incapacity for routine work for more than seven consecutive days | ~ Within 15 days of the accident | HSE |
| Dangerous occurrence, reg. 7 and Sch. 2 para. 10 | An accident or incident which results or could have resulted in release or escape of a biological agent "likely to cause severe human infection or illness" | ✓ Notify without delay, report within 10 days | HSE |
| Record only, reg. 12(1)(c) | Incapacity for routine work for more than three consecutive days | ✗ No report due | Kept by the practice |

Two points of detail decide most real cases. HSE's own guidance says that "severe human infection or illness" in that Schedule 2 category refers to biological agents in Hazard Groups 3 and 4 of Schedule 3 to COSHH, which is where hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV sit, so a stick from a source known to carry one of them is the case that reaches HSE.

And regulation 12(2) sets the retention period that practices usually get wrong: a record kept under regulation 12(1) "must be kept for at least three years from the date on which it was made".

> **Most needlestick injuries in a dental practice are reportable to nobody and recordable by you.** No absence, no known infection, no RIDDOR report. The regulation 7(1) record is still compulsory, and it is the only document that will ever tell you that three injuries in a year happened at the same point in the same procedure.

## The question you will be asked, and where the answer lives

The occupational health clinician will not begin with the wound. They will ask who the source patient was, at what time, and what procedure was running, because that is what sets the risk and what consent for source testing has to be sought against.

That information is almost never in the accident form. It is in the day's appointment book and in that patient's clinical record, and under pressure, twenty minutes of hunting for it is twenty minutes of the window gone.

## The principle that makes the rest work

The 2013 Regulations put information duties on the employer through Schedule 1 and training duties through Schedule 2, and regulation 6(2) requires the employer to "cooperate with worker representatives" in developing that information.

In a practice of five people that has one practical meaning. If a stick is treated as the carelessness of whoever got stuck, it stops being reported, and the practice loses the only data that would have moved the sharps bin or paid for safer needles.
