Ask three things before anything else: how you get your data out, what you will pay in total over three years, and who else can read your patients' clinical records. A demo shows the product on its best day. Those three answers tell you what year three looks like, which is exactly when switching has become expensive.

None of the three is about features. Features are what the demo covers, and most of the market has the same ones.

## How you leave, asked before you arrive

The question is literal: if I leave tomorrow, what do I take, in what format, and how long does it take?

- **What format everything comes out in.** CSV, XML or a database you can query without their licence. A PDF per patient is not an export, it is a filing cabinet.
- **Who generates the export.** Pulling it yourself from the admin panel whenever you like is not the same as emailing a request and waiting for someone to prepare it.
- **Whether images and radiographs are included** at their original resolution, not as a compressed thumbnail.
- **Whether it costs money.** A fee for walking away with your own records is an answer in itself, whether or not you accept it.
- **How long they keep your data** after you close the account, and when it is deleted.

*This is what an export has to preserve on the day you leave: every entry with its date, not just today's state.*

> **Ask for a test export during the evaluation, not on your way out.** It is the only way to confirm it exists and that it opens. A vendor who shows you the export without flinching is telling you something, and so is one who talks around it.

If you do end up switching, the order you do it in matters as much as the format: that is covered in [how to migrate without losing the clinical record](/en/blog/migrate-dental-software/).

## What the price includes and what is billed separately

Year one is rarely the problem. Year three is, with two more chairs and three modules you did not need at the time.

- **What unit you pay for**: per user, per chair, per practice or per patient volume. That changes the total far more than the headline rate.
- **Which modules sit outside the plan**: invoicing, reminders, imaging, digital signature, reporting. Ask for a closed list of what is not included.
- **Setup and migration**: whether they are one-off charges, how much, and exactly what that figure covers.
- **Support**: whether it is in the price or a plan sold on top.
- **Term and increases**: how many months you are committed for, how it renews, and how much notice they need to raise the rate.
- **What growth costs**: two more chairs and a new hygienist, and what that adds.

> **Ask for the three-year total in writing, based on the growth you actually expect.** Not the monthly rate: the total. A vendor who will not put that in an email has a reason, and the reason shows up on the year-two invoice.

## Who owns the data and where it lives

- **Where it is physically hosted**, in which country and on whose infrastructure.
- **Who else can reach it**: which companies act as subprocessors, and for what.
- **Whether you can take a copy yourself**, whenever you want, without asking permission.
- **What happens if they close or get acquired**: who is answerable for your data, and for how long.
- **What the contract lets them do with your data** beyond providing the service to you.

Ask for the data processing agreement and the subprocessor list in writing too. What you specifically need in your jurisdiction is a question for your own adviser or data protection officer, not for the person selling to you.

## What happens when something breaks at nine on a Monday

- **The real support hours**, not the ones on the website. If you open at eight and they open at nine, that hour is yours to cover.
- **How you reach them**: phone, email or a ticket queue. A contact form with no phone number is a product decision, not a detail.
- **Who answers**: someone who has seen dental practices, or a general helpdesk that will ask you for screenshots.
- **What response time they commit to**, in writing rather than in conversation.
- **When they deploy updates** and whether you get told first. An update during surgery hours is a problem.
- **What happened the last time it went down.** Ask it exactly like that, and watch whether the answer is ready.

## The questions, and what a bad answer sounds like

| What to ask | An answer worth having | An answer to worry about |
|---|---|---|
| How do I export everything? | ✓ Open format, you run it | ✗ "We'll sort that out if you leave" |
| What does three years cost? | ✓ A figure in writing | ✗ "It depends on a lot of things" |
| Which modules are extra? | ✓ A closed list | ✗ "We can look at that later" |
| Where is my data held? | ✓ A named country and provider | ✗ "In the cloud" |
| What support do I get? | ✓ Hours and a response time | ✗ "We're very responsive" |
| How long is the term? | ✓ Months and notice period, written | ✗ Automatic renewal with no reminder |

None of the answers on the right means the product is bad. They mean you still do not have the information, and signing without it is the avoidable part.

## How to get something out of the demo

1. **Bring your own case**, not theirs. A real quote, a treatment you do every week, a patient with a long history.
2. **Ask for the keyboard.** Watching a salesperson who has used it for two years tells you nothing about how long it will take you.
3. **Time the things you do twenty times a day**: booking an appointment, taking a payment, printing a treatment plan. That is where the end of your day is won or lost.
4. **Ask for the export during the demo itself** and open it in front of them.
5. **Put the questions in writing** and keep the answers by email. What gets said on a call does not travel with the contract.

A "no" to something on this list does not rule a vendor out. One who says "we don't do that" is easier to evaluate than one who says yes to everything.
