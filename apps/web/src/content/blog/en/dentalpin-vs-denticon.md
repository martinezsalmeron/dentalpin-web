Denticon is built for dental groups. Planet DDS says so on the product page itself, calling it "the practice management system built to help DSOs scale", and the rest of the product follows from that sentence.

We make Dentalpin, so we are not neutral. What we can be is accurate.

> **How this comparison is sourced.** Every claim about Denticon below comes from a page Planet DDS publishes on planetdds.com, linked and dated at the end. No aggregator blogs and no review sites: they contradict each other and some are written by competitors. There is a whole section on when Denticon is the better choice, because for a lot of practices it is.

## In thirty seconds

**Denticon** is a cloud system for multi-location dentistry, from Planet DDS, a company that has been at this since 2003. Imaging, revenue cycle management, analytics, payments and live AI agents sit around it, the conversion off your old system is staffed, and the platform is stated to be SOC 2 Type 2 and HIPAA compliant.

**Dentalpin** is open source and costs nothing to license: no fee per chair, per dentist or per patient, the code published, the records on hardware you choose. In exchange it is from 2026, somebody has to own the server, and it has no imaging and nothing for US insurance.

The question that decides between them is how many locations you are running. Denticon is priced, built and sold for groups. If you are one practice with three chairs, you are not its centre of gravity.

## What Denticon is

Cloud practice management software published by Planet DDS, a company that describes itself as making "dental software built to help DSOs improve efficiency and increase revenue".

The company dates itself to 2003 and says Denticon "was among the first cloud-based dental practice management systems". It has grown by acquisition since: Apteryx, the imaging line, in 2020, and Cloud 9, for orthodontics, in 2023. Those products plus Planet DDS Pay and MyTooth are sold together as **DentalOS**.

The Denticon feature list on its own page is wide, and most of it is aimed at running many offices at once:

- **Clinical.** AI Voice Perio and AI Voice Restorative Charting, multi-specialty functionality, and integrated dental imaging with AI.
- **Front office.** Scheduler, Quick Fill, automated appointment messaging, two-way messaging, online booking and digital forms, internal and external referral management.
- **Money.** Revenue cycle management and insurance, AutoEligibility, automated balance due reminders and Planet DDS Pay.
- **Enterprise.** Analytics, reporting and data share, single sign-on, advanced security, and Task Manager across locations.

For the group buyer the pitch is centralisation, in their words: "From 5 offices to 300+, centralization is key."

### What it runs on, and this is the surprise

Denticon is browser-based, and its own system requirements answer is narrower than that sounds.

> **"Denticon is a browser-based cloud software that only operates on Windows operating systems."** The same answer adds that Denticon "is not currently compatible with Mac operating systems" (consulted 6 September 2026). A cloud product that still rules out half the machines at the front desk is worth knowing about before you buy the Macs.

### What Denticon costs

This is one of the rare vendors in any market that publishes a figure, so credit where it is due.

Their FAQ says: "Denticon practice management software starts at $795 per month, with customizable packages, features, and add-ons based on your practice's specific size and needs."

Two things that sentence does not say, and neither can be filled in from anywhere else on their site: whether $795 is per office or per organisation, and what the base includes before the add-ons. Their own pricing article carries no figure at all and points you to the sales line instead.

There is also a published discount programme. **Launchpad** offers "more than 80% in savings during year 1" to new clients with two to nine locations, annual revenue between $2M and $12M, a growth plan, and no venture capital or private equity backing. From year two, standard pricing applies.

## What Dentalpin is

Open source dental practice management software. You download the code, install it wherever you want (your own server, whichever cloud you pick), and pay no licence per chair, per dentist or per patient.

Odontogram, periodontogram, scheduling, clinical records, treatment plans, quotes with signature capture, invoicing, payments, recalls and reports. Plus an AI assistant that carries out tasks against your real data, bounded by each user's permissions.

It runs in any modern browser, on Windows, macOS or Linux. What it does not have is imaging, ePrescribe or anything for US insurance, and it is from 2026 with none of Planet DDS's two decades of accumulated edge cases.

## Choose Denticon if

This section is meant seriously, not as a formality.

- **You run more than one location.** Centralised scheduling, billing and reporting across offices is the entire design brief, and it is not a thing you bolt on later. Dentalpin handles multiple sites, but nothing we do is built for 300 of them.
- **US insurance is the daily grind.** Revenue cycle management, AutoEligibility and claims work are in the product. Nothing we do touches this, and no amount of open source fixes it.
- **You want imaging from the same vendor.** Apteryx is device-agnostic, AI-enabled, with unlimited cloud storage and integrations for the major sensor brands. We have no imaging module at all.
- **Someone has to answer to a compliance review.** SOC 2 Type 2 and HIPAA compliance are stated for DentalOS. We claim no certifications, and saying otherwise would be a lie you would find out about later.
- **You want somebody else to run the conversion.** They publish an average of 60 days for a solo practice and 8 weeks per location for a DSO, with the work done in the background while you keep seeing patients.
- **You want AI agents working today.** Confirmation, recall and cancellation-rescheduling agents are listed as available now, not coming soon.

If you are a growing group with two to nine offices, ask them about Launchpad before you ask anyone about anything else. An 80% first-year discount changes the arithmetic of this whole comparison.

## Choose Dentalpin if

- **Your front desk is not all Windows.** Denticon's own system requirements rule out macOS. Ours run in any modern browser on any operating system.
- **You want the price before you talk to anyone.** Ours is on a page: [what it costs](/en/pricing/). Nothing per chair, per dentist or per patient, and no starting-at asterisk.
- **You want the records on hardware you control.** Their pages do not say where patient data is hosted. Here you pick the server, the provider and the jurisdiction.
- **You want to audit the code** that holds your clinical records, or have someone audit it for you. It is published.
- **The bill should not grow every time the practice does.** Opening another chair should not change what you pay, and one more location should not either.
- **You want to integrate freely on a system you own.** A documented REST API with OpenAPI, against a database you can reach directly.

## What migrating looks like

Planet DDS runs the conversion for you. Ours is a tool you drive yourself, and the difference is real work that lands on somebody.

The `migration_import` module imports through [dental-bridge](https://github.com/dentaltix/dental-bridge), and it is deliberately not one button:

1. **Upload the file** and the system validates it before touching anything.
2. **See a preview** with entity counts and sample rows. Nothing has been written yet.
3. **Review the proposals**: the system maps the source treatment catalogue onto yours and you decide row by row (accept, relink, create new, or ignore). Anything scoring above 0.9 can be accepted in bulk.
4. **Execute**, and the import runs honouring your decisions.

> Step 3 is where most migrations go wrong. Two practices never code treatments the same way, and **a silently guessed equivalence produces invoices that are wrong in ways nobody notices for months**.

If you would rather hand the whole migration to a project team, that is a genuine reason to pick them.

## The honest part

For a US dental group billing insurance across a dozen offices, Denticon is the better fit today, and this comparison is not going to pretend otherwise. The centralisation, the RCM, the imaging and the staffed conversion are exactly what that buyer needs, and we have none of it.

The picture flips for a single practice, especially outside the United States. There the case for Denticon rests on infrastructure you may not need, and the Windows-only requirement in their own FAQ is a hard constraint that most cloud products no longer impose.

Dentalpin is the other bet: that the software holding clinical records should be readable and ownable by the practice it holds them for. It is younger and it shows. You can [try the demo](https://demo.dentalpin.com) without installing anything, or [stand it up on your own server in three minutes](/en/blog/install-dentalpin-in-three-minutes/) and judge it yourself.
