Dentrix is the default answer in the United States, and the numbers on its own site explain why: "35,000+ Dental practices use Dentrix everyday" and "30+ Years of continuous innovation and leadership". What its site does not say, anywhere we could find, is what it costs.

We make Dentalpin, so we are not neutral. What we can be is accurate.

> **How this comparison is sourced.** Every claim about Dentrix below comes from a page Henry Schein One publishes on dentrix.com, linked and dated at the end. No aggregator blogs and no review sites: they contradict each other and some are written by competitors. There is a whole section on when Dentrix is the better choice, because for many readers it is.

## In thirty seconds

**Dentrix** is the incumbent, and it has the install base, the integration ecosystem and the onboarding department to match: 35,000+ practices by its own count, 150+ connected technology partners, and clinical AI built into its imaging. If you practise in the United States and want the thing everyone already knows how to use, this is it.

**Dentalpin** is open source and costs nothing to license: no fee per chair, per dentist or per patient, the code published, the data wherever you decide to put it. In exchange it is from 2026, it is self-hosted, and it does not do imaging.

The question that decides between them is not really features. It is whether you are willing to book a sales call to find out the price, because with Dentrix that is the only way to find out.

## What Dentrix is

Practice management software from Henry Schein One, sold in two separate products that its own comparison page sets side by side.

- **Dentrix** is the on-premises one. "Installed locally on office computers", with data on "Local servers (practice-managed)", and its site positions it for "Solo and small group practices".
- **Dentrix Ascend** is the cloud one. "Accessed via web browser from anywhere", positioned for "All practice types, including multi-location and DSOs".

The same page is candid about the trade: "Ascend requires no local servers or IT maintenance. Dentrix needs regular updates and IT support."

Around that core sits a wide product surface, described on their solutions pages: insurance, billing and collections; charting, imaging and clinical AI; marketing and patient experience; practice data and analytics; and managed IT support. Dentrix API Exchange is described as "A community of software vendors helping Dentrix customers expand their capabilities."

The clinical AI is not a roadmap item. Their charting page describes Dentrix Imaging with Image Verify, "real-time, AI-powered caries detection in Dentrix Imaging" that "instantly highlights potential decay", and voice-powered documentation producing "complete SOAP notes and narratives in real time, as you speak". Hands-free clinical charting is listed as coming soon.

Onboarding is a staffed process rather than a download: a project manager runs the data conversion, the practice tests in a trial environment with its own data, and staff get full-day hands-on training before launch.

### What Dentrix does not publish

> **Dentrix does not publish prices on its site (consulted 7 August 2026).** The packages and suites page names no price and carries no figure at all. It offers "Request a Demo" and "Contact Us" instead, so that Dentrix can help determine "which capabilities will accelerate your practice's growth".

The onboarding and conversion page is the same: it describes the process in detail and names no fee, and it names no source products it converts from either. Both are worth knowing before you plan a switch, and neither is a criticism. It is simply how this vendor sells, and it is how most of this industry sells.

We are not going to fill that gap with a number from a blog. If the price matters to you, and it should, ask them directly and get it in writing.

## What Dentalpin is

Open source dental practice management software. You download the code, install it wherever you want (your own server, whichever cloud you pick), and pay no licence per chair, per dentist or per patient.

Odontogram, periodontogram, scheduling, clinical records, treatment plans, quotes with signature capture, invoicing, payments, recalls and reports. Plus an AI assistant that carries out tasks against your real data, bounded by each user's permissions.

It runs in a browser, so no workstation needs to be a particular kind of machine. It is also from 2026, with none of Dentrix's thirty years of accumulated edge cases, and it has no imaging module at all. Both of those matter, and we come back to them.

## Choose Dentrix if

This section is meant seriously, not as a formality.

- **You practise in the United States and insurance is the daily grind.** Eligibility, claims and collections built for that market, refined over decades. Nothing we do touches this.
- **You want imaging and clinical AI in the same product.** Dentrix Imaging with caries detection and voice-driven SOAP notes are in the box. Dentalpin has no imaging module, and pretending otherwise would help nobody.
- **You want someone to run the migration and train the team.** A project manager, a test environment loaded with your own data, and a full day of hands-on training is a real service, and switching software is where practices lose weeks.
- **Your equipment and third-party tools already integrate.** 150+ connected partners is an ecosystem you would be walking away from.
- **You want the cloud without running anything.** Dentrix Ascend exists for exactly that. Dentalpin is self-hosted: someone has to own the server and the backups.
- **Nobody in the practice is technical, and nobody wants to be.** That is a legitimate position and it points at Dentrix.

A product with 35,000+ practices behind it has solved problems we do not yet know exist.

## Choose Dentalpin if

- **You want to know the price before you talk to anyone.** Ours is on a page: [what it costs](/en/pricing/). Nothing per chair, per dentist or per patient.
- **You are uneasy about clinical data living somewhere you do not control.** Here you pick the server and the jurisdiction.
- **You want to audit the code** that holds your clinical records, or have someone audit it for you. It is published.
- **You want to integrate and automate freely.** A documented REST API with OpenAPI on a system you control, rather than a partner programme you apply to.
- **The bill should not grow every time the practice does.** Opening another chair should not change what you pay.
- **You practise outside the United States**, where the insurance workflow that makes Dentrix strong is not the workflow you have.

## What migrating looks like

The `migration_import` module imports through [dental-bridge](https://github.com/dentaltix/dental-bridge), and it is deliberately not one button:

1. **Upload the file** and the system validates it before touching anything.
2. **See a preview** with entity counts and sample rows. Nothing has been written yet.
3. **Review the proposals**: the system maps the source treatment catalogue onto yours and you decide row by row (accept, relink, create new, or ignore). Anything scoring above 0.9 can be accepted in bulk.
4. **Execute**, and the import runs honouring your decisions.

This is a self-service tool, not the staffed conversion Dentrix describes. If you want somebody else to own the migration end to end, that is a genuine reason to pick them.

> Step 3 is where most migrations go wrong. Two practices never code treatments the same way, and **a silently guessed equivalence produces invoices that are wrong in ways nobody notices for months**.

## The honest part

Dentrix is the incumbent for defensible reasons: thirty years, 35,000+ practices, imaging and clinical AI in the product, an integration ecosystem, and people who will run your migration. If you are a US practice that wants the safe choice, it is the safe choice, and this comparison is not going to pretend otherwise.

Where we would push back is the price. A practice should be able to compare two products without booking a call, and today it cannot do that with Dentrix. Ours is published, the code is published, and you can check both before speaking to anyone.

Dentalpin is the other bet: that the software holding clinical records should be readable and ownable by the practice it holds them for. It is younger and it shows. You can [try the demo](https://demo.dentalpin.com) without installing anything, or [stand it up on your own server in three minutes](/en/blog/install-dentalpin-in-three-minutes/) and judge it yourself.
