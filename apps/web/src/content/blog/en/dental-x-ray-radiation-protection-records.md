An inspection of radiation protection does not ask about the X-ray set, it asks for the paperwork the X-ray set generates. In a UK dental practice that is a registration with the Health and Safety Executive, a radiation risk assessment, local rules, a contingency plan, the employer's procedures required by IR(ME)R, evidence of adequate training for everyone who justifies or takes an exposure, and the equipment test results. Then there is the part that lives inside the patient record rather than in a folder: who justified each exposure, and why.

Below is each of those with the regulation it comes from, how long it is kept, and the half almost no article covers, which is what practice management software can actually produce and what it cannot.

This is not legal advice and it does not replace your radiation protection adviser or medical physics expert. Sources and the dates they were consulted are at the end.

## Two regulations, two different jobs

Dental radiography in the UK sits under two instruments that people routinely confuse, and they protect different people.

- **The Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017** (SI 2017/1075, IRR17) protect staff and the public. This is where registration with HSE, the risk assessment, controlled areas, local rules and personal dose monitoring come from.
- **The Ionising Radiation (Medical Exposure) Regulations 2017** (SI 2017/1322, IR(ME)R17) protect the patient. This is where justification, optimisation, the employer's procedures and adequate training come from.

Those are UK instruments. Radiation protection is national everywhere, and an article that gives you one global answer is wrong somewhere. Ireland, the United States and Australia each set their own regime, and the principle below holds even where the paperwork differs: the record is what makes the exposure defensible.

## Registration comes before the first exposure

The 2020 Guidance Notes for Dental Practitioners, published by Public Health England with the Faculty of General Dental Practice, put it plainly: before commencing work with dental X-ray equipment for the first time, the employer must register their work with a radiation generator with HSE.

Three details from that guidance are worth having in front of you when you do it:

1. **The application goes to HSE's dedicated service** for England, Scotland and Wales, and to HSENI in Northern Ireland, and it carries a fee everywhere except Northern Ireland.
2. **Several of the declarations require an RPA.** The registration asks the employer to declare that specific requirements of IRR17 have been met, and meeting them means appointing and consulting a suitable radiation protection adviser.
3. **Questions about expected dose mean you took a wrong turn.** The guidance notes that if the form starts asking about expected staff dose or about REPPIR19, the employer has selected a category of work requiring a consent, which is not appropriate for routine dental X-ray work.

> **Working without a registration is an offence, not a backlog item.** The guidance tells existing practices that have yet to register under IRR17 to do so as soon as possible, in exactly those terms.

## What has to be recorded for each exposure

This is the part that touches the patient record, and it is narrower than most people assume.

The identity of the IR(ME)R practitioner who justifies and authorises the exposure **must** be recorded. Where a patient is referred between practices, for CBCT imaging for example, the names of the referrer and of the operator who will report on the images should be recorded too.

Exposure factors are the surprise. Where an examination follows a guideline exposure protocol, the factors do not need to be recorded at all. Where you deviate from those standard settings, the guidance says to record the settings used, the reason for the deviation and the total number of exposures taken at those settings, so a dose estimate can be made later if it is ever needed.

> **A radiograph sitting in a folder on a disk is not a record.** It has no author, no indication and no date you can defend. The regulation is satisfied by the line in the notes, not by the image file.

## The employer's procedures, and the file they live in

IR(ME)R17 requires the employer to have written procedures, and Schedule 2 lists them. In a dental practice they cover entitlement and training of duty holders, referrals, justification and authorisation, patient identification, pregnancy enquiry, information on benefits and risks, carers and comforters, clinical evaluation, assessment of patient dose, diagnostic reference levels, reducing the probability of accidental exposures, and what to do when one happens.

The guidance recommends keeping master copies of all of it, plus the risk assessment, local rules and contingency plans, in one **radiation protection file**, held in hard copy or electronically, kept up to date and accessible to everyone who needs it during day to day work or an inspection.

It also recommends a procedures log recording each occasion on which those documents are reviewed or modified and issued to staff, with reviews whenever circumstances change or otherwise **at intervals not exceeding three years**, and a version number or date on every page.

## Testing intervals and retention periods

Two tables' worth of dates that are easy to let slip. The testing intervals come from chapter 5 of the guidance, the retention periods from its table 6.1.

| Record or activity | Interval or retention | Where it comes from | Produced by practice software? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routine testing of X-ray equipment | Normally not exceeding three years | Guidance Notes 5.2.3 | ✗ Done by an external tester |
| Annual testing instead | Hand-held sets always, plus doses persistently over the NDRL | Guidance Notes 5.2.3 | ✗ External |
| Review of procedures and local rules | Not exceeding three years | Guidance Notes 5.1.1 | ~ As a document store |
| Record of critical examination | Working life of the equipment | IRR17 | ✗ External |
| Record of registering with HSE | Until changed or cancelled | IRR17 | ✗ Administrative |
| Personal dose monitoring, non-classified staff | At least two years | IRR17 | ✗ From the dosimetry service |
| Staff training in radiation safety | At least five years | IRR17 | ~ As a staff record |
| Controlled area hand-over forms | At least three years | IRR17 | ✗ Paper |
| Justification and identity of the practitioner | With the patient record | IR(ME)R17 | ✓ A clinical entry |

The right-hand column says something uncomfortable and true. **Most of radiation protection is not a software problem.** It is external certificates, external tests and a folder. Any system that claims to cover all of it is claiming too much.

One more line from that table deserves reading twice: IR(ME)R17 requires numerous records but **specifies no retention period for them**, and the guidance's advice in the absence of one is to keep records, including QA checks and audits, and the employer's procedures for at least five years, with records of staff training in radiation safety kept beyond that.

## Training is a five-year cycle, not a one-off

IR(ME)R17 requires that any practitioner or operator is adequately trained for the role they play, and that they undertake continuing education after qualification. On top of that, the General Dental Council recommends at least **five hours of verifiable CPD in radiography and radiation protection in each five-year CPD cycle** for dentists and dental care professionals who carry out radiography.

That turns into a practical rule for the practice: the list of people entitled to justify or take an exposure is a named list, and each name on it has a training date that expires. A new hygienist who can place a sensor is not, by that fact, entitled.

## The part that genuinely depends on the software

Three questions that an inspection, a complaint or a claim can ask, which your record system either answers or does not:

- **Why was this radiograph taken?** There has to be a dated line in the notes with the indication behind it, attributable to the person who justified it.
- **Who justified it and who took it?** These are two different people more often than people expect. If the system only records a shared login, the answer does not exist.
- **How many exposures has this patient had?** That question requires counting, not browsing. If radiographs are loose files in a directory, counting means opening folders one at a time.
