Three free things, in this order: claim and verify your Google Business Profile and fill it in completely, put your address, opening hours and treatments on your own site as readable text, and ask every patient for a review using the same sentence. Google states in writing that there is no way to pay for a better position in local results. Everything else is optional, and most of what an agency sells comes after this, not instead of it.

What follows is how to do each of the three, what a dental regulator will let you claim, and the four numbers that tell you whether any of it worked.

## Google ranks on relevance, distance and prominence

The Business Profile help page explains the criteria in three words, and they are worth reading before spending anything. Relevance is *"how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for"*. Distance is *"how far each business is from the customer who's searching"*. Prominence *"means how well-known a business is"*.

Two of the three are outside your control. Distance is set by the patient when they search from their kitchen, and prominence accumulates over years. Relevance is the one you can fix in an afternoon, and you fix it by filling the profile in.

> **Google says it flatly: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google".** No budget moves the map pack. Anyone selling you positions in it is selling something their own supplier denies exists.

## Filling the profile in without getting it suspended

Google's guidelines for representing a business are short, and they get broken for the same reason every time: the temptation to stuff keywords into the name field. The policy asks that the name *"should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers"*, and it explicitly excludes taglines, service descriptors, phone numbers and URLs.

Categories work the opposite way to what most people assume. Google asks you to *"Use as few categories as possible to describe your overall core business from the provided list"*, and to pick categories that describe what the business is, not what it offers.

| What you put in the profile | What Google's policy says |
|---|---|
| The name on the sign above the door | ✓ Exactly what the policy asks for |
| That name plus "Implants & Invisalign Leeds" | ✗ Service descriptor, not allowed |
| Primary category "Dentist" | ✓ Describes what the business is |
| Ten categories, one per treatment | ~ Policy asks for as few as possible |
| The premises address, with permanent signage | ✓ Real location, signposted |
| A virtual office or an unsigned home address | ✗ Fails the signage requirement |

The steps, once, in this order:

1. **Search for the practice on Google Maps before creating anything.** Profiles are often already there, generated automatically or claimed by a previous practice manager, and a duplicate is worse than no profile at all.
2. **Claim it and verify it.** Google says verification *"tells Google that you're authorized to represent the business, so it's more likely to show up in search results"*.
3. **Enter the exact name on the sign**, with nothing appended.
4. **Pick one primary category** and at most two secondary ones that are true every day of the year.
5. **Enter the real hours**, bank holidays included, and correct them the day they change.
6. **Upload photographs of the actual practice**: the frontage, reception, one surgery. The frontage is the one that helps someone find the door.
7. **Check that somebody answers the phone** during the hours you published. It is the most expensive failure on this list and the easiest to detect.

## Look at the gaps before you go looking for patients

Bringing more people to a diary that is already full changes nothing, and bringing them to Tuesday at eleven when what you have spare is Thursday evenings changes nothing either. Before touching any of the above, open the last four weeks of the schedule and look at when the gaps actually are.

![Practice schedule in day view, with appointments arranged by surgery and the free gaps between them](/screenshots/schedule-day.png)

*Day view of the schedule: each column is one surgery, and the blank spaces are the unbooked slots.*

That changes what you write. If the spare capacity is early mornings, the profile hours and the line on your homepage should say so. If the spare capacity is a hygiene chair, the problem is not marketing, it is recall.

## What a regulator will let you claim

Advertising by dental professionals is regulated in every market this site publishes in, and the body doing the regulating changes with the border. In the United Kingdom it is the General Dental Council, and its guidance on advertising is the clearest published statement of the rules in English.

The GDC requires a practice website to display *"the name and geographic address at which the dental service is provided"*, *"contact details of the dental service, including e-mail address and telephone number"*, *"the GDC's address and other contact details, or a link to the GDC website"*, *"details of the practice's complaints procedure and information about who patients may contact if they are not satisfied with the response"* and *"the date the website was last updated"*. Each clinician named on the site needs their professional qualification, the country it was obtained in, and their GDC registration number.

On claims, the guidance is short and unambiguous. You must avoid *"statements or claims intended or likely to create an unjustified expectation about the results you can achieve"*, and you must not *"exploit the trust, vulnerability or relative lack of knowledge of your patients"*.

> **"Specialist" is a protected word, not a compliment you can pay yourself.** Only dentists on a GDC specialist list may use it or a title like Orthodontist or Periodontist. Everyone else uses "special interest in", "experienced in" or "practice limited to", which the GDC names as the acceptable alternatives.

This is not legal advice, and if you practise outside the UK the specific list will differ: check your own regulator's advertising guidance before the copy goes live, not after.

## The website: what a neighbour needs to find in thirty seconds

It does not need rebuilding. It needs these things present as text, not inside an image or a PDF, because nothing inside an image is read by anyone searching.

- **The full address and how to get there**, worded exactly as it is on the Business Profile. Two spellings of the same street is two businesses competing with each other.
- **The hours, matching the profile.** A contradiction here is the fastest way to send someone to the practice next door.
- **A tappable phone number** at the top, above the fold, on a phone.
- **One page per significant treatment**, two or three hundred words written by somebody who works there. A person searching "root canal" wants to know what it involves, how long it takes and whether it hurts.
- **Who works there**, with names, qualifications and registration numbers, as the guidance above requires.
- **Prices, if you are willing to publish them.** Almost nobody does, which is exactly why the page gets shared when it exists.

## The patients you already have are worth more than the ones you do not

This is the part no agency sells, because it does not bill as a campaign. A patient who has already been in, already trusts you and whose number you already hold costs one message.

- **Reviews get asked for from everyone, in the same words.** How to do that without crossing a line is in [dental reviews on Google](/en/blog/google-reviews-dental-practice/).
- **Recall is local marketing**, and the cheapest there is. It is in [dental recall](/en/blog/dental-recall-system/).
- **Inactive patients are a list, not a feeling.** The criteria for building it are in [reactivating inactive patients](/en/blog/reactivate-inactive-dental-patients/).
- **Half-accepted treatment plans** are patients who already said yes to something. Following them up is in [following up dental quotes](/en/blog/follow-up-dental-treatment-quotes/).

## The four numbers that tell you whether it worked

Monthly, five minutes, and not one of them needs a paid tool.

![Practice reports screen showing the indicators for the selected period](/screenshots/reports.png)

*The reports screen: indicators for the selected period, grouped in blocks.*

1. **New patients who booked**, and where they say they heard of you. The question gets asked at reception on the first call and written into the record.
2. **Missed calls.** If twenty calls come in a month and six go unanswered, marketing is not your problem.
3. **New reviews, and unanswered ones.** The second figure should be zero.
4. **Unfilled slots in the diary**, against last month. It is the only number here that measures a result rather than an effort.

## The two weeks it takes

1. **Day 1.** Find the profile on Maps, claim it, and start verification, which takes a few days to come back.
2. **Day 2.** Name, category, hours and phone number, corrected and then checked from a phone that is not on the practice wifi.
3. **Day 3.** Photographs of frontage, reception and surgery, taken on a phone on a day the practice is tidy.
4. **Day 4.** Run the website against the list above and fix what is missing, address and phone number first.
5. **Day 5.** Write the sentence the team will use to ask for a review, and leave it at reception.
6. **Week 2.** One treatment page a day, written by the person who does that treatment.
7. **First Monday of the following month.** The four numbers on one sheet. The comparison only starts to exist in month two.
