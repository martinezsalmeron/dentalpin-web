Software of Excellence has been selling dental software since 1989 and calls EXACT "The UK's number one dental practice software", and the thing a buyer notices first is that their team installs it into your practice for you and that nowhere on their site is there a price.

We make Dentalpin, so we are not neutral. What we can be is accurate.

No aggregator blogs and no review sites: they contradict each other and some are written by competitors. There is a whole section on when EXACT is the better choice, because for a lot of practices it is.

## In thirty seconds

**EXACT** is the long-established incumbent. Founded in New Zealand in 1989, in the UK since 1994, part of Henry Schein since 2007, and by its own account "the market leader with over 4,500 dental practice customers" as far back as 2009. It comes with imaging, AI on radiographs, NHS contract prediction and a named consultant who phones you, and their team installs and migrates it for you.

**Dentalpin** is open source and costs nothing to license: no fee per surgery, per dentist or per patient, the code published, the data on hardware you choose. In exchange it is from 2026, nobody installs it for you, it has no imaging and it does no NHS claim work at all.

The question that decides between them is whether you want a supplier or a system. If you want somebody to specify the hardware, convert the data and coach the practice, that is what they sell. If you want to read the code and hold the keys, that is what we sell.

## What Software of Excellence is

Its product is EXACT, described on its own solutions page as "the complete practice management system you already know".

Its published history is unusually specific, and worth reading before anyone calls them old:

- **2002.** "This was a landmark year as we reached our 1000th customer in the UK."

Henry Schein One's own product page states the market plainly: "Software of Excellence is available in the UK, Australia and New Zealand." There is also an Irish site at the same domain.

### The modules, which is where the product actually lives

EXACT is sold as a core system with a long list of separately named products around it, and their own descriptions are the clearest guide to what each one is for:

- **Recall Manager** "Ensures that the recall process runs seamlessly, keeping your appointment book full."
- **Utilisation Manager** is "Designed to make best use of all available surgery time by recognising when an appointment is booked within a defined period."
- **Care Manager** "Provides a clear view of all outstanding treatments in one single dashboard."
- **Workflow Manager** is "Customised to your practice settings to ensure that patients are always rebooked, whilst identifying outstanding debt and incomplete patient details."
- **Short Notice Lists** "enable critical appointment gaps to be automatically filled by patients who'd like to be seen sooner."
- **Marketing Manager** "Allows you to create, schedule and send your marketing campaigns."

Alongside those sit Examine Pro for imaging, which "Seamlessly integrate[s] digital images into one single database", My Practice Cloud for analytics you "Access ... from any device", MPC Pay for "automated associate dentist payments", Dentist Portal, Patient Portal, Clinipad, Online Booking, Chairsyde and Second Opinion AI.

That is a genuinely deep catalogue. What their site does not publish is which of those arrive with EXACT and which are bought separately, so that is a question for the call rather than a fact we can state here.

### The NHS part

For a UK practice this matters more than anything else on the page. EXACT's NHS pitch is built around prediction rather than paperwork: "Track your NHS contracts with our Predictive UDA Manager" and "Get intelligent predictions for your practice and individual dentists and hit your NHS contract targets".

Their own description of the UDA Manager is that it is "A key feature for NHS practices, it provides intelligent predictions for the practice and individual dentists, so that you remain on target for each contract". They also claim "96% of practices in our Customer Success Programme hit their NHS contracts".

> **Their NHS page does not name which of the four nations it covers.** Consulted on 17 August 2026, it talks about contracts and UDAs without stating England, Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, and mentions no claim submission route by name. If you work in Scotland or Northern Ireland, get that in writing before you sign.

We should be equally unambiguous in the other direction. Dentalpin submits nothing to the NHS anywhere.

### Where it runs

Their own onboarding description settles the deployment question without any inference needed. "Our team will remotely install EXACT into your practice, add your Examine Pro imaging software and any value-added products."

The hardware is part of the sale too. "If you're purchasing new equipment, we'll go over system requirements with you", "If you're using existing equipment, we'll guide you through a quick system check", and they offer "Preconfigured, tested and professionally installed IT equipment from the most trusted partner in dentistry".

So the records sit on a machine in the practice, which is closer to how we work than a cloud-only rival is. The difference is not where the data lives, it is who can open the software that holds it.

## What Dentalpin is

Open source dental practice management software. You download the code, install it wherever you want (your own server, whichever cloud you pick), and pay no licence per surgery, per dentist or per patient.

Odontogram, periodontal charting, scheduling, clinical records, treatment plans, quotes with signature capture, invoicing, payments, recalls and reports. Plus an AI assistant that carries out tasks against your real data, bounded by each user's permissions.

It runs in any modern browser, so no workstation has to be a particular kind of machine. What it does not have is imaging, radiograph AI or NHS claim work, and for a mixed or NHS practice that last one is the end of the conversation.

## Choose Software of Excellence if

This section is meant seriously, not as a formality.

- **You do NHS work.** UDA prediction against contract targets is the daily reality of an NHS practice, they have built it, and we have not. No amount of open source makes up for a contract you miss.
- **You want somebody else to own the switch.** Their migration team "will connect to your server to export your data", run a test trial, then hold a "Trial Review with your team and our Customer Success Consultant to verify that the data is accurate". That is a service, not a download.
- **You want imaging from the same supplier.** Examine Pro puts digital images "into one single database" with the records, and Second Opinion by Pearl reads radiographs. We offer neither, and pretending otherwise would help nobody.
- **You want the hardware handled.** They will check your machines or sell you preconfigured ones. A practice with no IT contact should weigh that heavily.
- **You want a person, not a forum.** Their Customer Success Programme is "a one-to-one relationship with your very own dental business coach", and they claim "92% of customers told us the programme had either a significant or major impact on business outcomes". We have GitHub and a chat channel.
- **You want training on site.** "Training is available both remotely and on-site." Ours is documentation.

A product with that many practices behind it has solved problems we do not yet know exist.

## What migrating looks like

Theirs is a managed service and it is described above. Ours is a tool you drive yourself, and it is deliberately not one button:

1. **Upload the file** and the system validates it before touching anything.
2. **See a preview** with entity counts and sample rows. Nothing has been written yet.
3. **Review the proposals**: the system maps the source treatment catalogue onto yours and you decide row by row (accept, relink, create new, or ignore). Anything scoring above 0.9 can be accepted in bulk.
4. **Execute**, and the import runs honouring your decisions.

The `migration_import` module imports through [dental-bridge](https://github.com/dentaltix/dental-bridge).

> Step 3 is where most migrations go wrong. Two practices never code treatments the same way, and **a silently guessed equivalence produces invoices that are wrong in ways nobody notices for months**.

If you would rather a migration team did all four steps and sat in a review call with you afterwards, that is a real reason to pick them.

## The honest part

For an NHS practice in the UK, EXACT does work we have not done, and a comparison that buried that would be worthless. The same goes for imaging, for radiograph AI and for the simple fact that somebody arrives, installs the thing and trains the staff.

What we would push back on is narrower. A catalogue of separately named managers with no published price and no published breakdown of what is included makes it genuinely hard to know what you will pay, and a practice cannot compare two quotes it has to phone for. Their marketing claims, the 48% private revenue growth and the 96% NHS contract attainment, are theirs and are not independently verifiable.

Dentalpin is the other bet: that the software holding clinical records should be readable and ownable by the practice it holds them for. It is younger and it shows. You can [try the demo](https://demo.dentalpin.com) without installing anything, or [stand it up on your own server in three minutes](/en/blog/install-dentalpin-in-three-minutes/) and judge it yourself.
