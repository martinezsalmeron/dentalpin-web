A periodontal chart records, at six sites per tooth, the probing depth and whether that site bleeds, plus the position of the gingival margin so attachment level can be worked out, and per tooth the furcation, the mobility and any teeth lost to periodontitis. You read it by comparing dated series, never a single number.

What follows is what belongs in each cell, why probing depth on its own misleads, and what the software has to do so the chart you record in three years is still comparable with today's.

## What gets recorded, site by site and tooth by tooth

Six sites per tooth means three buccal and three palatal or lingual: mesial, mid and distal. The British Society of Periodontology makes a distinction that is usually read backwards: when a full chart is indicated, all six sites must be **measured** even though only those of 4 mm and above need to be **recorded**.

| Measurement | What it answers | Unit of record |
|---|---|---|
| Probing depth | How far the probe goes into the sulcus | Six sites per tooth |
| Gingival margin or recession | Where the gingiva sits relative to the CEJ | Six sites per tooth |
| Clinical attachment level | How much support has actually been lost | Derived from the two above |
| Bleeding on probing | Whether that site is inflamed today | Six sites, yes or no |
| Suppuration | Whether there is active infection at that site | Six sites, yes or no |
| Plaque | Whether plaque control explains the inflammation | Six sites, yes or no |
| Furcation involvement | Whether the lesion reached the root bifurcation | Per furcation entrance |
| Mobility | How much support is left overall | Per tooth |
| Tooth lost to periodontitis | It feeds the stage, it is not just a gap | Per tooth |

> **Bleeding is not an optional column.** The BSP's BPE guidance puts it plainly: bleeding on probing should always be recorded in conjunction with a six-point pocket chart. A run of depths with no bleeding data cannot tell you whether a pocket is active or stable.

## Probing depth on its own misleads

Two mouths with the same number can be in opposite situations, which is exactly why attachment level exists.

- **With recession, a shallow pocket hides a large loss.** A tooth with 3 mm of recession and a 3 mm pocket has lost 6 mm of attachment, and the depth column presents it as healthy.
- **With swollen, inflamed tissue the reverse happens.** The margin has migrated coronally, the probe travels 5 mm and the attachment may be intact.
- **That is why the case definition rests on interdental attachment loss**, not on depth. The 2017 world workshop defines a periodontitis case as interdental CAL detectable at two or more non-adjacent teeth.

Recording the gingival margin costs one extra cell per site, and it is the only thing that turns a table of depths into a history of the supporting tissues.

## Screening tells you whether to chart, not what the diagnosis is

A screening index and a full chart answer different questions, and running them together is the most common charting mistake. The British Society of Periodontology says so in writing: the BPE is for screening only and should not be used for diagnosis.

Screening is done by sextant with a WHO probe, 0.5 mm ball end and a black band from 3.5 mm to 5.5 mm, using a light probing force of 20 to 25 grams.

| Code | What is seen | What it triggers |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pockets < 3.5 mm, no calculus or overhangs, no bleeding | ✓ No periodontal treatment needed |
| 1 | Pockets < 3.5 mm, no calculus, bleeding on probing | Oral hygiene instruction |
| 2 | Pockets < 3.5 mm, supra or subgingival calculus or overhangs | Remove plaque retentive factors |
| 3 | Probing depth 3.5 to 5.5 mm | ~ Six-point chart in that sextant, after initial therapy |
| 4 | Probing depth > 5.5 mm | ✗ Six-point chart throughout the dentition |
| * | Furcation involvement | Recorded alongside the code, for example 3* |

Three practical consequences the same guidance sets out, and that almost no software reflects:

- **Screening cannot measure the response to treatment**, because it says nothing about how sites within a sextant changed. That needs a six-point chart before and after.
- **In maintenance, full probing depths are recorded throughout the dentition at least annually**, not just in the sextant that caused trouble.
- **Sextant screening is not used around implants.** Four or six-point charting is used instead.

## How the trend is read: staging and grading

The 2018 classification, from the joint workshop of the American Academy of Periodontology and the European Federation of Periodontology, separates two questions that used to travel together: how much has already been lost, and how fast it is being lost.

| | Stage I | Stage II | Stage III | Stage IV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interdental CAL at worst site | 1 to 2 mm | 3 to 4 mm | ≥ 5 mm | ≥ 5 mm |
| Radiographic bone loss | Coronal third (< 15%) | Coronal third (15% to 33%) | To the middle third and beyond | To the middle third and beyond |
| Teeth lost to periodontitis | None | None | ≤ 4 | ≥ 5 |
| Maximum probing depth | ≤ 4 mm | ≤ 5 mm | ≥ 6 mm | ≥ 6 mm |

Extent is described separately: localised when under 30% of teeth are involved, generalised, or a molar-incisor pattern.

Grade estimates the speed. The indirect criterion is percentage bone loss divided by age: under 0.25 is grade A, 0.25 to 1.0 is grade B, above 1.0 is grade C. Smoking and diabetes modify it, with 10 cigarettes a day and an HbA1c of 7.0% as the thresholds. The classification itself says to assume grade B at the outset and look for evidence to shift to A or C.

> **Grade is a division, and the software holds both numbers.** Percentage bone loss over patient age is a sum no program should be asking a clinician to do by hand, and nearly all of them do. Where direct evidence exists, meaning two dated radiographs or two dated charts, that evidence outranks the indirect calculation.

## The number that closes a course of treatment

The European Federation of Periodontology's S3-level clinical practice guideline sets a concrete target for the supportive phase: no pockets greater than 4 mm that bleed on probing. That is the criterion that makes a chart more than documentation.

1. **Residual pockets of 4 to 5 mm**: repeat non-surgical subgingival instrumentation.
2. **Residual pockets of 6 mm or more**: access flap surgery.
3. **Intrabony defects of 3 mm or deeper**: regenerative surgery.
4. **Supportive care**: visits at intervals of 3 to a maximum of 12 months, tailored to the patient's risk profile and their condition after active therapy.

The first three are decisions taken by reading specific cells of the re-evaluation chart. If the software cannot list the sites still over 4 mm with bleeding, that reading gets done by eye off a paper chart.

## What the software has to do so the series stays comparable

- **Fix the order of the sites.** If one clinician records mesial to distal and another the other way round, tooth 16 ends up with two series that cannot be laid over each other.
- **Store who probed.** Inter-examiner variation is real, and without the examiner you cannot interpret a 2 mm jump between two recalls.
- **Never overwrite the previous measurement.** A chart that holds only the current state deletes the very data it exists for.
- **Calculate attachment level rather than asking for it.** Depth plus recession is a sum the program should do.
- **Give a full-mouth bleeding score**, not just the individual marks. That is the figure compared between visits.
- **Link the radiograph the bone measurement came from**, from the chart itself and not from a separate folder.
- **Export everything with its dates.** A chart that exists only inside one vendor's program is a screen, not a series.

## How to test yours in ten minutes

1. **Open a long-standing periodontal patient** and ask to see the trend at the mesiobuccal site of tooth 16. If all you can see is the latest reading, you have no series.
2. **Record 2 mm of recession** and check that the attachment level is calculated for you.
3. **Mark bleeding at several sites** and look for the full-mouth percentage. If it is not there, you will be counting by hand at every recall.
4. **Find where the furcation grade is stored** and whether it distinguishes the separate entrances of an upper molar.
5. **Change a depth on purpose** and check that the change is logged with date and user, and that the previous value is still readable.
6. **Ask for the list of sites over 4 mm with bleeding** from the last chart. That is the query that decides the next step of treatment.
7. **Export the full history** in a format you can open without the program, and see whether every dated measurement comes out or only the last one.

Point 7 is the one everybody postpones and the only one that cannot be fixed on the day it matters.
