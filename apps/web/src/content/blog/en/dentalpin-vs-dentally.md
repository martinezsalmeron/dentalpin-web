Dentally is one of the few dental systems in any market that tells you what it costs before you speak to anyone, and the price list rewards a close reading: £125 a month for a single-surgery site on Starter, £575 at eight surgeries, and digital imaging only on the most expensive of the three plans.

We make Dentalpin, so we are not neutral. What we can be is accurate.

> **How this comparison is sourced.** Every claim about Dentally below comes from a page Dentally publishes on dentally.com, linked and dated at the end. No aggregator blogs and no review sites: they contradict each other and some are written by competitors. There is a whole section on when Dentally is the better choice, because for a lot of practices it is.

## In thirty seconds

**Dentally** is cloud-native practice management built for the NHS. It states full integration with the NHS in England, Wales and Scotland and with HSC in Northern Ireland, it has been going since 2013, Henry Schein One has owned it since 2020, and its own site puts "70,000+ practitioners" across five countries behind it. It also publishes its prices, which most of this industry will not do.

**Dentalpin** is open source and costs nothing to license: no fee per surgery, per dentist or per patient, the code published, the data on hardware you choose. In exchange it is from 2026, somebody has to own the server, it has no imaging, and it does no NHS claim submission at all.

The question that decides between them is whether you do NHS work. If you are submitting claims in any of the four nations, Dentally has built that and we have not, and nothing else in this comparison outweighs it. If you are wholly private, the decision moves to who holds the records and what you pay as the practice grows.

## What Dentally is

Cloud-only dental practice management software, described on its own home page as "The market leading cloud solution for faster, simpler dental practice management". There is no on-premises version and no local server, which is the design rather than a gap.

The company was "founded in 2013 by Nick and James", Nick Davies and James Harker, and "was acquired in 2020 by Henry Schein One". It is based "in the UK in Gillingham, Kent with offices internationally in Canada, Australia and New Zealand", and the footer names the trading entity as Henry Schein One UK LTD, company registration 02940919.

On scale, its own story page states "70,000+ practitioners across the UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia and New Zealand use Dentally", and the same page puts the total at "over 80,000 worldwide" after the New Zealand launch in August 2025. Both figures are theirs.

The product splits into three published names:

- **Dentally**, the practice management system itself.
- **Dentally Vision**, digital imaging, which "bring[s] clear, high-quality imaging and intelligent clinical insights together in one effortless workflow" alongside Dentally Vision AI.
- **Dentally Portal**, the patient-facing side, listed on the price table as "Digital Forms, Online Appointment Booking, Kiosk and Concierge".

### What it does

On the clinical side its own feature pages describe charting as "a comprehensive overview of every patient's oral state, with tools like mixed dentition and root view allowing you to customise your chart screen", and periodontal charting under "Perio, perfected": "Record and review periodontal exams in seconds. See changes over time with intuitive visuals and side by side comparisons".

Clinical notes are dictated rather than typed. Dentally describes "Clinical AI notes turns your dictations into clear, structured notes using your existing templates", and treatment planning offers "pre-built plans for those re-occurring treatments".

On the front desk it publishes "a single customisable calendar, a clear visits management panel", "real-time reporting" giving "insights into practice income, outstanding treatment plans, practitioner activity", automated SMS and email reminders, and family accounts covering "managing payments to processing claims and deposits for families or individuals".

### The NHS part, which is the real moat

This is where a UK comparison is won or lost, and Dentally is unambiguous about it: "Fully integrated with NHS in England, Wales, Scotland and HSC Northern Ireland".

What it publishes underneath that is specific rather than a badge. "Real-time reporting gives you instant visibility of your NHS and HSC activity - from UDA progress to contract tracking and outstanding claims." For England and Wales it lists "automatic fee updates to simple claim submissions with exemptions handled for you", for Scotland "supporting CHI and SIMD lookups, GP17 digital signing, prior approvals, and exemptions", and for Northern Ireland "automatic fee updates to keep you compliant".

We should be equally unambiguous in the other direction. Dentalpin submits nothing to the NHS.

### What it costs

> **Dentally publishes a full tariff, and almost nobody else in this market does.** The pricing page carries a slider, "How many surgeries do you have per site?", running from one to nine, and three plans priced against it. Every figure below is from that page, excluding VAT.

At one surgery per site the plans are **Starter £125 a month, Essentials £220 and Pro £320**. At three surgeries they are **£265, £380 and £510**. At eight, the highest the slider prices, they are **£575, £720 and £945**. Move the slider to nine and every plan says "Contact us" instead.

Users and storage are unlimited on all three plans, so the meter runs on surgeries and not on headcount. That is a genuinely different shape from a per-user licence, and for a practice with a lot of part-time associates it is the friendlier one.

### What the plan you were quoting is missing

The interesting part of a published price list is which rows are unticked, and here the answer is not the one the tier names suggest.

- **Digital imaging is on Pro only.** Dentally Vision is unticked on Starter and unticked on Essentials. A practice that assumed imaging arrives with the middle plan is looking at £510 a month rather than £380 at three surgeries.
- **Dentally Portal starts at Essentials.** Digital forms, online booking, kiosk and concierge are not part of Starter.
- **Integrations are "Approved partners" on Starter and Essentials**, and "Custom" only on Pro. Custom email domains are Pro only too.
- **Support is "Chat" on Starter**, and "Chat & Phone" on Essentials and Pro. A customer success manager comes with Essentials, a dedicated one with Pro.
- **AI clinical notes are metered**: a "Limited trial" on Starter, "45 Clinical note transcriptions per surgery, per month" on Essentials and 90 on Pro. The row also states they are "not available for Northern Ireland & Republic of Ireland".

Multi-site capability, NHS integration, internal practice chat and postcode lookup are ticked on all three plans, including Starter.

### Where it runs, and how you connect to it

"All you need is Google Chrome." Dentally states that it "runs entirely in your web browser, with all data stored securely on remote servers", with "no on-site server", "no per-device licensing", and no "expensive server room, IT maintenance contracts, and complicated installations". If the line goes down, it says "You can continue using Dentally with a mobile data connection (3G/4G)".

On security its page states "End to end encryption: We protect your data with strong encryption in transit and at rest", "Only you have the key: Your data stays confidential and in your control", two-factor authentication, per-person access levels, control over "exactly where and when your data can be accessed", and real-time activity monitoring.

> **What that page does not say is where the data physically lives.** Consulted on 15 August 2026, Dentally's security page names no hosting provider, no region, no ISO 27001, no Cyber Essentials and no NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit status, and publishes no backup schedule or uptime figure. Those answers may well exist; they are simply not on the page, so ask for them in writing.

### The API

Dentally runs a partner marketplace with named integrations across imaging and infrastructure, patient communication, payments and finance, practice growth, and reporting, workflows and compliance. Stripe, Worldpay, Dojo, Xero and Mango Voice are among them.

The access rule is published plainly: "API access is only available to approved Dentally integration partners." There is an API and there are keys, and getting to it means becoming a partner rather than reading the docs and starting.

Odontogram, periodontal charting, scheduling, clinical records, treatment plans, quotes with signature capture, invoicing, payments, recalls and reports. Plus an AI assistant that carries out tasks against your real data, bounded by each user's permissions.

## Choose Dentally if

This section is meant seriously, not as a formality.

- **You do any NHS or HSC work.** UDA tracking, exemptions, automatic fee updates, GP17 digital signing in Scotland, CHI and SIMD lookups. We do none of it, and no amount of open source makes up for a claim you cannot submit.
- **You want to see the price before the call.** They publish it, on a slider, with the plan contents next to it. That is rare enough in this market that it deserves saying twice, and it is the thing we most want other vendors to copy.
- **Unlimited users suits how you staff.** If you run associates, hygienists and part-time reception, a per-surgery meter with unlimited logins is likely cheaper and much simpler to predict than a per-seat licence.
- **You want imaging and practice management from one supplier.** Dentally Vision is in the same product, on the Pro plan. Dentalpin has no imaging module and pretending otherwise would help nobody.
- **Nobody should have to think about a server.** No on-site hardware, updates daily with "no downtime or disruption", and someone to phone from Essentials up.
- **You want dictated clinical notes now.** Their AI turns dictation into structured notes on your existing templates. Our assistant works on your data on request, which is a different thing.

A product with 70,000+ practitioners behind it has solved problems we do not yet know exist.

## Choose Dentalpin if

- **You are wholly private and the records matter more than the claims.** Everything above about the NHS stops applying, and the question becomes who holds the data.
- **You want the records on hardware you control.** Dentally does not publish where its data is hosted. Here you pick the server, the provider and the jurisdiction, and you can point at the machine.
- **Losing the connection cannot mean losing the day.** Dentally needs a connection, with mobile data as the fallback. A local install keeps running on your own network.

## What migrating looks like

Dentally publishes no conversion counts or migration timetable that we could cite, so ask them for both. Ours is a tool you drive yourself.

The `migration_import` module imports through [dental-bridge](https://github.com/dentaltix/dental-bridge), and it is deliberately not one button:

1. **Upload the file** and the system validates it before touching anything.
2. **See a preview** with entity counts and sample rows. Nothing has been written yet.
3. **Review the proposals**: the system maps the source treatment catalogue onto yours and you decide row by row (accept, relink, create new, or ignore). Anything scoring above 0.9 can be accepted in bulk.
4. **Execute**, and the import runs honouring your decisions.

> Step 3 is where most migrations go wrong. Two practices never code treatments the same way, and **a silently guessed equivalence produces invoices that are wrong in ways nobody notices for months**.

If you want somebody else to own the migration end to end, that is a genuine reason to pick them.

## The honest part

For a UK practice doing NHS dentistry, Dentally is the better answer and this comparison is not going to pretend otherwise. The claim workflows across four nations are years of unglamorous work, they are the daily reality of the job, and we have not done them.

What we would push back on is narrower. A price list is a real service to buyers, and theirs is undermined a little by imaging sitting on the top plan, so the number a practice first quotes itself is often not the number it will pay. And a security page that promises "Only you have the key" while naming no hosting region, no certification and no backup schedule is asking for more trust than it evidences.

Dentalpin is the other bet: that the software holding clinical records should be readable and ownable by the practice it holds them for. It is younger and it shows.
