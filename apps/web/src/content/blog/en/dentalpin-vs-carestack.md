We make Dentalpin, so we are not neutral. What we can be is accurate.

## What CareStack is

Cloud practice management software published by Good Methods Global, Inc., trading as CareStack, with its US headquarters in Celebration, Florida and offices in Trivandrum, Kochi and Bangalore.

Their company page tells the story plainly: "Dr. Mark Huzyak and Abilash Krishna founded CareStack in 2015", when Dr. Mark was running three dental groups in central Florida. The platform "was launched commercially to the general public in late 2017". The same page publishes four figures: $145M raised, 10 years in business, 1,300+ employees, 3,000+ practices.

It sells into three markets, each with its own site: the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia. The UK version carries a whole NHS module set (NHS Centre, NHS Reporting, NHS Claims Management, NHS Compliance) that does not appear on the US one.

The product is genuinely all-in-one, and the feature table on their pricing page is the best evidence for it:

- **Front office.** Multi-specialty scheduling, short call list, appointment finder, digital patient forms, custom consent forms, check-in kiosks and a patient self-service portal.
- **Clinical.** Treatment charting, perio charting, integrated imaging in the chart, clinical notes with templates, treatment planning, and ortho, perio and oral surgery case management.
- **Money.** Membership plans, payment plans with auto-debit, pay by text or email, electronic statements, and PCI-compliant card storage.
- **Insurance.** Real-time eligibility, electronic claims with scrubbing, attachments with custom rules, full life-cycle claim tracking, ERAs with automated posting, and rejection and denial workflows.
- **Analytics.** Provider and location scorecards, goal tracking, trend analysis, custom patient lists and audit trails.

Certifications are stated on the home page: "CareStack is fully compliant with HIPAA and possesses SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001:2022 certifications."

### What CareStack costs

The pricing page is headed "All-in-one Dental Platform. Transparent Pricing." and it carries two plans.

**Essentials** is "Starting at $829 / month" and covers scheduling, charting and treatment planning, native imaging, the patient portal and forms, eligibility, claims management, bulk texting, lab cases and team chat. **Intelligence** is "Starting at $1299 / month" and adds four things: AI-powered clinical notes, AI-powered voice perio charting, and X-ray analysis and clinical diagnosis by Overjet.

Credit where it is due, and then the two qualifications, both of which they also publish.

Those four AI rows are the only ones marked "No" on the cheaper plan. In the whole feature comparison table, perio charting, ortho and oral surgery case management, ERAs, denial workflows and audit trails all sit inside Essentials. Most vendors gate the clinical core, CareStack does not, and that is a point in their favour.

The first qualification is the words "starting at". The FAQ on that same page says: "We offer flexible pricing plans based on a combination of factors, including number of locations, chairs, providers etc., Please book a free demo with us to discuss more about the customized pricing options we can provide to your practice." So $829 is a floor for a shape of practice the page does not define.

The second is geography. The UK pricing page carries no figure at all, only the same invitation to book a demo, so a practice in Leeds gets none of the transparency a practice in Orlando does.

There may also be a setup cost: "Depending on the complexity of data migration and additional training requirements for your office, we may charge an implementation fee in certain cases."

Licensing is not per seat, which matters for a busy front desk: "CareStack license is usually set against number of locations, providers etc., This means that you can add users without having to buy new licenses for each of them."

### They publish API prices too, and this is rare

CareStack runs a developer portal with "a rich set of RESTful APIs covering all practice and patient related resources", plus webhooks and scheduled data extracts. Under the heading "Predictable Pricing, No Surprises" it publishes the tariff: a one time registration of $5,000.00, a minimum fee per month of $100.00, and $60.00 per location per month for 10,000 transactions, then $0.004 for every additional transaction.

Almost no vendor in any market publishes what its API costs. This one does, and the figure is worth reading twice before you plan an integration around it.

## Choose CareStack if

This section is meant seriously, not as a formality.

- **US insurance is the daily grind.** Real-time eligibility, claim scrubbing, electronic attachments, ERAs with automated posting and denial workflows are all in the base plan. Nothing we do touches any of it, and no amount of open source fixes that.
- **You are an NHS practice.** Their UK product carries NHS claims, reporting and compliance as named modules. We have nothing there at all.
- **You want imaging and e-prescribing from the same vendor.** FDA-cleared native imaging sits inside the chart, and their prescribing "supports most of the pharmacy chains across the United States". We offer neither.
- **You want AI diagnostics that exist today.** X-ray analysis and clinical diagnosis by Overjet, plus voice perio charting, are shipping features on the Intelligence plan, not a roadmap.
- **Somebody has to answer to a compliance review.** HIPAA, SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001:2022 are stated on their own site. We claim no certifications, and saying otherwise would be a lie you would find out about later.
- **You want support with published hours.** "Monday through Friday from 7:00 am to 8:00 pm; Saturday and Sundays are available from 7:00 am to 3:00 pm", with a stated guarantee of reaching a live representative "in under one minute". We do not offer that, and pretending otherwise would not help you.
- **You want somebody else to run the conversion.** Their team has "successfully converted data from over 60 practice management software systems" and describes the cutover as the office logging out one evening and finding its patients in CareStack the next morning.

## The exit, in their own words

Their contract is clear that the data is yours: "Customer shall retain all right, title and interest in and to the Customer Data, including all intellectual property rights therein." That sentence is better than a lot of what we read in this market, and it should be said first.

What follows it is the part to read before signing. Under the same SaaS Customer Agreement, getting a copy out works like this:

1. **You ask in writing**, at least 10 days before the date you want the export.
2. **CareStack picks the format**, "in a format selected by CareStack (in its discretion)".
3. **You pay for it**, "at Customer's sole expense", at "its then-current hourly rates".
4. **They aim to deliver within 15 days**, on commercially reasonable efforts.
5. **The window closes**, at 90 days from expiry or termination.

> **An hourly rate with no published number is not a price you can plan around.** It is the one line in this comparison that we would want to see quoted in writing before signing anything, and it is worth asking them what a full export of a two-surgery practice has actually cost recently.

The term itself renews on its own: the initial length is whatever the Order Form says (their site does not publish it), and after that it "shall automatically renew for additional successive periods of one year each" unless either side gives notice "no later than thirty (30) days prior" to the renewal date.

For what it is worth, day-to-day export is easier than the termination clause suggests: their FAQ says custom patient lists "can be exported as .csv files" for use in a spreadsheet. That is a report, not your clinical record, and the two should not be confused when you are planning an exit.

## What migrating looks like

CareStack runs the conversion for you, with a team that has done it from more than 60 systems. Ours is a tool you drive yourself, and the difference is real work that lands on somebody.

The `migration_import` module imports through [dental-bridge](https://github.com/dentaltix/dental-bridge), and it is deliberately not one button:

1. **Upload the file** and the system validates it before touching anything.
2. **See a preview** with entity counts and sample rows. Nothing has been written yet.
3. **Review the proposals**: the system maps the source treatment catalogue onto yours and you decide row by row (accept, relink, create new, or ignore). Anything scoring above 0.9 can be accepted in bulk.
4. **Execute**, and the import runs honouring your decisions.

> Step 3 is where most migrations go wrong. Two practices never code treatments the same way, and **a silently guessed equivalence produces invoices that are wrong in ways nobody notices for months**.

If you would rather hand the whole migration to a project team, that is a genuine reason to pick them.

## The honest part

For a US practice that lives on insurance, or a UK practice doing NHS work, CareStack is the better fit today and this comparison is not going to pretend otherwise. Eligibility, claims, ERAs, imaging, e-prescribing and NHS reporting are the daily job in those practices, and we have none of it.

The picture changes for a private practice outside those two systems, and it changes again if you think about the exit before the entry. Their pricing page is more open than most of this market. Their termination clause is not, and an hourly rate for a copy of your own records is a real cost that never appears in a monthly comparison.

Dentalpin is the other bet: that the software holding clinical records should be readable and ownable by the practice it holds them for. It is younger and it shows.
